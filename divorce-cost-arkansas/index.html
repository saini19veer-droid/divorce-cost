📜 What to Expect When You File in Arkansas

Divorce procedure varies by state in the details, but the overall arc in Arkansas looks like this:

  1. Petition filed. One spouse submits the paperwork to open the case, paying the court's filing fee at this step.
  2. Spouse notified. The responding spouse must be formally served and given a legal window to reply.
  3. Assets and income disclosed. Both spouses share a full financial picture — this stage is where attorney and appraisal fees often start piling on.
  4. Negotiation phase. Whether through direct talks or a mediator, this is where most divorces actually get resolved.
  5. Decree issued. With terms finalized — by agreement or court order — the judge signs the divorce into effect.

Treat this as a general framework rather than Arkansas-specific legal guidance — a licensed attorney or your local courthouse self-help center can confirm exact requirements for your case.

Divorce Costs in Arkansas: The Full Breakdown

The cost of divorce in Arkansas ranges from roughly $2,280 for a simple uncontested case to $25,600 or more for a contested divorce that goes to trial. The single biggest factor is not the state — it is how much you and your spouse disagree.

Arkansas requires a 30-day waiting period. Fault grounds are still recognized.

How to Reduce Your Divorce Costs in Arkansas

  • Reach agreement before engaging attorneys. The difference in Arkansas between arriving with a rough agreement and arriving with nothing is typically $4,880–$12,200 in fees.
  • Explore mediation before litigation. Couples who mediate usually finish in 4–6 sessions for roughly $2,200–$6,200, compared to $10,000–$40,000 when the case heads to court.

📊 Typical Cost Example in Arkansas

A couple in Little Rock with no children using fault grounds would typically spend $165 in filing fees and $1,000–$2,500 in attorney costs — roughly $1,200–$2,700 total. Using the 18-month separation no-fault route adds waiting time but keeps costs similar for simple cases.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions: Arkansas Divorce Costs

How much does a divorce cost in Arkansas?

An uncontested divorce in Arkansas typically costs $3,800–$5,700 including the $165 filing fee. A contested divorce averages $16,000–$25,600 per spouse depending on attorney hours and complexity.

What are the grounds for divorce in Arkansas?

Arkansas allows no-fault divorce after 18 months of separation. Fault grounds — which allow faster filing — include adultery, felony conviction, habitual drunkenness, and cruel treatment.

What is the waiting period for divorce in Arkansas?

The standard no-fault waiting period requires 18 months of separation. If filing on fault grounds, there is no mandatory separation period but the process still takes several months.

How is property divided in an Arkansas divorce?

Arkansas courts divide marital property equitably — fairly but not necessarily equally — based on factors such as length of marriage, each spouse's economic circumstances, and contributions to the marriage.
